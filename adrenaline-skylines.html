POW!
In development · Solo project

Adrenaline
Skylines

A chaotic skydiving race game, like Mario Kart in free fall. Everyone drops toward the ground at once, diving and spreading to control their speed and slamming into rivals on the way down.

I'm designing, coding and drawing it myself in the Godot game engine, with the goal of releasing it on Steam someday.

EngineGodot 4 (GDScript)
ArtHand-painted in Krita
StatusWorking 2D prototype, 3D started

The idea

Every player wants to hit the ground first, but how you fall matters just as much as how fast. Two design ideas make the end of every race chaotic:

One parachute short

Every race spawns one fewer parachute than there are players. Land without one and you automatically finish last, no matter how fast you were. (Designed, not built yet.)

The Ring of Fire

A giant ring of fire marks the edge of the arena. It starts out of sight and closes in as everyone gets closer to the ground. (Designed, not built yet.)

What's working now

Dive & Spread

Hold Shift to dive (shrink down and fall faster) or Space to spread out and slow down. Speeding up helps you catch up; slowing down helps you line up with rivals or dodge them.

Floaty steering

Steering with WASD uses velocity, acceleration and drag, so it feels slippery, like you're actually falling through the air.

Mid-air collisions

Players only collide if they're at nearly the same altitude. Side-by-side hits knock both players apart, and diving into someone from above bounces them up or down the race.

Comic hit effects

Every hit flashes a comic-book "POW!" in a random color. It's drawn once in black and white and recolored in code, so one drawing gives endless color variations.

Faking 3D in a 2D game

The first prototype is 2D, but it creates the feeling of falling toward the ground using only scaling tricks:

The ground rushes up

The ground image grows as you fall. Growing it at a steady rate felt wrong, so I shaped a custom curve that starts slow and speeds up, which makes the fall look steady to the eye.

Rivals grow as they get close

Other players' size depends on how far above or below you they are. A rival diving past you balloons in size, then fades out "behind the camera," and comes back into view if you slow down.

Fair hitboxes

Hitboxes are kept separate from the visual size tricks, so collisions are fair for both players even when one looks much bigger on screen.

Layered character art

The character is drawn as separate body parts (core, arms, legs) with different poses for spreading and diving. Separate layers mean future cosmetics are just a texture swap.

Where it stands

The 2D prototype proved the core mechanics work, so I've started rebuilding the game in real 3D for better-looking characters and environments. Real 3D cameras replace all of the faked-depth tricks, and the race logic and menus carry over.

Things I learned the hard way

I built the game one mechanic at a time and kept a dev log the whole way. A few of the bugs I solved:

  • Players phasing through each other. Godot only reports a collision when two hitboxes first touch. If players were already overlapping before their altitudes lined up, they slipped right through. I fixed it by tracking overlaps and checking altitude every frame.
  • Players getting stuck inside each other. Allowing only one hit per overlap let players sit inside each other's hitbox. I replaced it with a short cooldown for each player.
  • Muddy knockback. Pushing players sideways and vertically at the same time felt messy, so I split collisions into two separate types based on altitude.
  • Invisible white pixels. Krita was baking a white background into exported art. Removing all white would have also erased the character's white underwear, so I only cleared the transparency in from the edges.
  • Choosing an engine. I compared Unreal Engine 5 with Godot and stayed with Godot. I already knew it well, it's faster to iterate in, it charges no royalties, and a cartoony game doesn't need Unreal's realistic graphics.
