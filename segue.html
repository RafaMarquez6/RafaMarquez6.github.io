Segue

Segue is a daily music game I designed, like Wordle but for playlists. Every day you get a start song and an end song that sound nothing alike. Your job is to build a playlist that blends smoothly from one to the other.

I built it with help from Claude, an AI assistant. It started as a prototype and is now a live website with accounts, a daily leaderboard, almost 70,000 songs and a paid Premium tier.

How it works

How to play

  1. Get today's pair. Everyone gets the same start and end song each day. The pair stays hidden until you start the clock.
  2. Build the bridge. You have 3 minutes to add 3 to 8 songs in between. Picks are final, and you get one run per day.
  3. Small steps score big. Each song is a dot on the sound map. Each transition scores up to 100 points depending on how close the two songs are, and going the wrong way costs 25.
  4. Spin the records. Every transition drops a little vinyl record colored by how smooth it was. Your row of records is what you share with friends.
  5. Or gamble on a Hard Cut. Make every step a huge jump that still lands closer to the end, and you can score up to 750. One smooth step ruins it.

What I built

The sound map and the DJ

Every song sits on a two-axis map: guitars to synths, and gentle to heavy. A little DJ character in Segue pink hops along your route as you add songs.

A 69,794-song catalog

Python scripts collected songs from Billboard charts and music databases. Claude placed about 69,600 of them on the map in one batch run for about $1.50, then re-checked the 22,000 most questionable placements.

Accounts and leaderboard

Players sign in with a password or a 6-digit emailed code, pick a DJ name, earn holiday badges and compete on the daily board, all stored in a Supabase database.

Premium for $3

A one-time $3 Stripe payment unlocks Infinite mode, with unlimited new pairs, and lets you customize your DJ's body, color, headphones, hat and shades.

Players can challenge scores

If a transition's score seems wrong, players flag it and say why. Every night, an automated job has Claude review the challenges and move misplaced songs.

Hosting and email

The site runs on Cloudflare at its own domain, seguedaily.com, and sends sign-in emails from its own address through Resend.
